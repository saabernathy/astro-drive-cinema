ORBIT 01 BLACK & WHITE
RELEASED 1951 • 20TH CENTURY FOX

The Day the Earth Stood Still

DIRECTED BY Robert Wise • MUSIC BY Bernard Herrmann
STARRING Michael Rennie, Patricia Neal, Hugh Marlowe
“Klaatu barada nikto!”

A flying saucer lands peacefully on the National Mall, only to be met by tank divisions and nervous military rifles. Robert Wise crafts the undisputed architectural high-water mark of Cold War pacifism. Bernard Herrmann’s pioneering dual-theremin score hums through the atomic atmosphere, while the gleaming eight-foot automaton Gort stands watch as a silent testament to galactic order.

★★★★★ GRADE A+
VERDICT: ESSENTIAL ATOMIC PARABLE
ORBIT 02 TECHNICOLOR
RELEASED 1953 • PARAMOUNT PICTURES

The War of the Worlds

PRODUCED BY George Pal • DIRECTED BY Byron Haskin
STARRING Gene Barry, Ann Robinson, Les Tremayne
“Guns, tanks, bombs... they're like toy popguns against them!”

George Pal radically modernized H.G. Wells into 1950s Southern California. Swapping tripod walkers for sleek, swan-necked copper war machines that emit searing green heat rays and red skeleton beams, the production is a feast of Mid-Century visual design. The sonic blast of the Martian death rays remains one of the era’s most distinctive audio achievements.

★★★★★ GRADE A
VERDICT: TRIUMPH OF MACHINE AGE FX
ORBIT 03 WARNERCOLOR
RELEASED 1954 • WARNER BROS.

THEM!

DIRECTED BY Gordon Douglas • STORY BY George Worthing Yates
STARRING James Whitmore, Edmund Gwenn, Joan Weldon
“We may be witnesses to a Biblical prophecy come true...”

The undisputed pioneer of the nuclear radiation mutation thriller. Set in the baking sands of the Trinity bomb site in Alamogordo, New Mexico, the procedural realism gives way to nine-foot irradiated ants. Gordon Douglas directs with intense documentary pacing, culminating in the claustrophobic flamethrower descent into the subterranean concrete storm drains of Los Angeles.

★★★★½ GRADE A
VERDICT: THE PREMIER ATOMIC MUTANT THRILLER
ORBIT 04 POLAROID 3-D
RELEASED 1954 • UNIVERSAL-INTERNATIONAL

Creature from the Black Lagoon

DIRECTED BY Jack Arnold • SUIT DESIGNED BY Milicent Patrick
STARRING Richard Carlson, Julie Adams, Ricou Browning
“We've merely scratched the surface of the unknown!”

Jack Arnold blends evolutionary biology with pulp terror in the Amazon basin. Milicent Patrick's Gill-man suit is a tour de force of creature fabrication, brought to life through Ricou Browning's graceful underwater choreography. The mirrored swimming ballet beneath Julie Adams remains one of the most hypnotic and poetically photographed sequences in the genre.

★★★★½ GRADE A-
VERDICT: MASTERCLASS IN ATMOSPHERIC AQUATICS
ORBIT 05 EASTMANCOLOR CINEMASCOPE
RELEASED 1956 • METRO-GOLDWYN-MAYER

Forbidden Planet

DIRECTED BY Fred M. Wilcox • SOUND BY Louis & Bebe Barron
STARRING Walter Pidgeon, Anne Francis, Leslie Nielsen
“Monsters from the Id!”

A stellar transplant of Shakespeare's The Tempest onto Altair IV. Featuring United Planets Cruiser C-57D, the pneumatic charm of Robby the Robot, and the subterranean 20-mile Krell reactors, this film defined the space-age aesthetic. Coupled with the world’s first entirely electronic tonal soundtrack, it laid the direct technological blueprint for modern space opera.

★★★★★ GRADE A+
VERDICT: SPACE AGE CINEMATIC ZENITH
ORBIT 06 SUPERSCOPE
RELEASED 1956 • ALLIED ARTISTS

Invasion of the Body Snatchers

DIRECTED BY Don Siegel • WRITTEN BY Daniel Mainwaring
STARRING Kevin McCarthy, Dana Wynter, King Donovan
“They're here already! You're next! You're next!”

The definitive manifestation of 1950s suburban dread. Alien seed pods sprout exact physical copies of the townsfolk of Santa Mira, stripping away emotion and love in favor of tranquil conformity. Don Siegel captures the quiet decay of American individuality with lean, breathless economy, closing on one of cinema’s most terrifying highway warnings.

★★★★★ GRADE A+
VERDICT: SUBURBAN PARANOIA MASTERWORK
